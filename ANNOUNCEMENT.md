**1/ (hook — attach `00-dashboard.png`)**

How healthy is Malaysia's democracy?

We built **Nadi Demokrasi** — a single page that measures seven decades of general elections (1955–2022)
with 14 standard political-science indicators. No hot takes, just numbers, every formula shown. 🧵

**2/ Votes vs. seats (attach `01-disproportionality.png`, `02-seat-bonus.png`, `03-malapportionment.png`)**

For 50 years, first-past-the-post turned modest vote leads into commanding majorities — the winner's
seat bonus hit **+27 points** in 1978. That reward has since vanished (−1 in 2022). But the gap between
votes and seats has a second source that *hasn't* gone away: **malapportionment** — unequal district
sizes — is now at a record **18%**, extreme by world standards.

**3/ Who the map favours (attach `04-map-bias.png`, `05-compactness.png`)**

We put two boundary questions on the page. The unequal map used to prop up the winner: in 2013 the ruling
bloc's seats held **22% fewer voters** than average — small rural seats it swept. By 2022 that flipped:
the winning bloc's seats held **32% *more* voters** than average, so the same map now works against
whoever wins the popular vote. And the latest redelineation drew the **least-compact boundaries on record**.

**4/ The end of one-party dominance (attach `06-dominance.png`, `07-fragmentation.png`, `08-multi-cornered.png`)**

The winning bloc's vote share has fallen from **82% (1955) to 38% (2022)**. The effective number of
parties has risen from ~1 to a record **3.6**, and the average seat now draws **4+ candidates**. Malaysia
has moved decisively from a one-and-a-half-party system to a genuine multi-bloc contest.

**5/ Movement & competitiveness (attach `09-volatility.png`, `10-turnover.png`, `11-marginal.png`)**

**53% of seats changed hands in 2022** — the highest churn on record. But vote volatility and seat
change aren't the same thing: 2008's "political tsunami" flipped many seats while relatively little of
the *vote* actually moved. And marginal seats — decided by under 5 points — are near record highs.

**6/ Who shows up, and who sits (attach `12-turnout.png`, `13-women.png`, `14-ethnicity.png`)**

Turnout has held between **69% and 84%** for seven decades. But the chamber still looks little like the
country it answers to: Parliament is **~13% women** (well below the ~30% many democracies treat as a
floor), and its ethnic mix has shifted markedly. Descriptive representation — who sits — not a verdict
on anyone.

([electiondata.my](https://electiondata.my)) — ~4 years of curated, CC0, peer-reviewed (*Nature*
Scientific Data) results. Every number on the page is recomputed from that data, so it updates itself.

Explore it — free, no login, mobile-friendly. Each indicator carries its formula and caveats:

## LinkedIn — single post

**How healthy is Malaysia's democracy? We tried to answer with numbers, not opinions.**

*Nadi Demokrasi* ("the pulse of democracy") is a free, single-page dashboard that measures seven decades
of Malaysian general elections (1955–2022) using fourteen standard political-science indicators —
disproportionality, malapportionment, two boundary/gerrymandering measures, the winner's seat bonus, the
effective number of parties, electoral volatility, seat turnover, marginal seats, turnout, and the
descriptive representation of women and ethnic communities in Parliament.

A few things the data shows:

• First-past-the-post once handed election winners a **+27-point** seat bonus; by 2022 that had vanished
  (−1). But unequal district sizes — **malapportionment**, now at a record 18% — mean votes still aren't
  worth the same everywhere.
• The unequal map itself once favoured the winner (its seats held **22% fewer voters** than average in
  2013); by 2022 that flipped to **32% more**, and the latest redelineation drew the least-compact
  boundaries on record — a first attempt at putting the gerrymandering question on the page.
• The winning bloc's vote share fell from **82% to 38%**, and the effective number of parties rose to a
  record **3.6** — the end of one-party dominance.
• **53% of seats changed hands in 2022**, the highest on record — yet vote volatility and seat change
  can tell very different stories (see 2008).

Every figure is computed from open data by one short, auditable script, and each indicator ships with its
formula and its limitations on the page — so it's a civic tool, not a verdict.

None of this is possible without **Thevesh Thevananthan's Malaysian Election Corpus** (electiondata.my) —
roughly four years of meticulous curation, released CC0 and peer-reviewed in *Nature*'s *Scientific Data*.
The data is the hard part; this just makes it legible.

Explore it (free, no login): https://zachtheyek.github.io/nadi-demokrasi/
